# Budget plan — Bảng B quadruped (hard cap 1,000,000 VND)

Status: budget reset requested by the team. This document supersedes the old 5,000,000 VND shopping plan for new purchases.

See [`BOM.md`](BOM.md) for the staged item-by-item purchase list, quantities, purchase gates and items explicitly deferred. The BOM is provisional until the exact controller, servo variant and competition rules are confirmed.

## Non-negotiable constraints

- Preserve the four-legged concept and 12 logical joints only if the required servos are already owned, borrowed, sponsored, or acquired in phases.
- No display, thermal camera, environmental sensor, online AI dependency, or decorative shell in the first competition build.
- Keep the physical emergency cutoff, fuse/protection, and separate servo power rail. Do not reduce electrical safety to meet the budget.
- Use existing school/team equipment first: printer time/filament, battery, wires, fasteners, controller and servos.
- Buy only after the exact board and servo models are confirmed.
- Do not claim a complete, reliable 12-servo robot can be built from zero for 1,000,000 VND.

## Cost reality

A current Vietnam retailer listing shows MG996R at about 85,000 VND before shipping/VAT, while other sellers list higher prices. Twelve at 85,000 VND already total 1,020,000 VND before the controller, power, frame, wiring or shipping. Price references: https://tae.vn/dong-co-rc-servo-mg996r and https://proe.vn/dong-co-servo-mg996r. Prices and stock can change.

Therefore, 1,000,000 VND is treated as a **new-purchase ceiling**, not proof that every part for a complete 12-servo robot fits inside it. If no servos or controller are already available, the complete 12-DOF design is not financially feasible under this ceiling without sponsorship/borrowed parts or a major change in robot architecture.

## Phase plan under the 1,000,000 VND new-purchase ceiling

### Phase A — inventory before buying (target spend: 0 VND)
Record exact model and quantity of:
- controller board (Huna Robo exact model, ESP32, or other)
- servos and horns
- servo driver, if any
- battery/regulated supply
- frame material / 3D printing access
- screws, wire, connectors, fuse and switch

### Phase B — minimum hardware proof
Build and test **one complete 3-DOF leg** before purchasing the remaining nine servos. Use this phase to validate the controller, PWM, power, mounting dimensions and inverse kinematics. Do not describe this phase as a competition-ready robot.

Indicative allocation only; replace with local quotes before purchase:
- up to 3 servos compatible with the selected mechanics: 255,000 VND at a reference 85,000 each
- controller: 0 VND if a suitable board is supplied/owned; otherwise quote first
- PWM driver: 0 VND if integrated/available; otherwise quote first
- power, fuse, switch and wiring: reserve about 250,000 VND
- frame, horns, fasteners and test prints: reserve about 250,000 VND
- contingency / shipping: keep at least 100,000 VND
- do not spend the remaining amount until the exact controller and servo rail requirements are known

This is a planning envelope, not a guaranteed quote or an approved purchase list. If a board/driver must be bought, recalculate the allocation before ordering.

### Phase C — complete robot
Only proceed when all 12 compatible servos and safe power components are available through purchases, existing stock, school support, borrowing or sponsorship. Prioritize:
1. safe standing
2. repeatable forward/backward gait
3. turning and lateral movement only if achievable and useful on the actual arena
4. task-specific sensors only after the mechanical task path is reliable
5. appearance and presentation after reliability

## Controller compatibility gate

The current C++ firmware is written for ESP32/Arduino APIs and currently uses ESP32-specific facilities. It is **not automatically compatible with a Huna Robo board just because that board accepts C/C++**.

Before porting firmware, record the exact Huna Robo model, processor/MCU, available PWM/I2C/serial interfaces, supported IDE/compiler and the competition rule about external boards. If the contest requires the official Huna Robo controller, retain it and port only what the documented SDK allows. If an external ESP32 is permitted, use ESP32 + PCA9685 as the reference path. Do not buy either board until that rule and model are confirmed.

## Firmware and CAD gates

- Hardware outputs remain disabled until the exact board, driver, servo voltage and channel map are verified.
- One servo → one leg → all legs; never first-test all 12 under load.
- Keep a physical power cutoff accessible; software E-STOP is not enough.
- CAD must preserve full-size servo and fastener interfaces. Never uniformly scale the whole robot to meet the envelope.
- Generated STL is not considered print-ready until its bounding box, mounting dimensions and clearances are checked against real parts.
- Report features as IMPLEMENTED, SIMULATED, PLANNED or HARDWARE-VERIFIED.
